About

From a vague request to the right problem. Nine years of research in experimental physics taught me how important it is to define the right question, especially when the answer doesn’t exist yet. A well-defined problem is half the solution; a wrong one can’t be solved, however hard you work.

  • Start from the goal. Good questions rarely arrive ready-made; they begin as a vague idea of what we want to achieve.
  • Let the evidence steer. Explore what the data and the analysis so far tell us, check that we’re measuring what we think, and use that to decide where to go next, until the question is sharp enough to answer.
  • Balance the trade-offs. With tight funding, schedules and teams, every step weighs speed, accuracy, scope and complexity.
  • Keep the long-term goal in view. A problem that moves us toward it deserves real investment; one that doesn’t may only need a quick fix until something better aligned replaces it.

What I’ve done. For a multi-region business growing through acquisitions, I rebuilt the analytics for the AI era: from fragmented, undocumented reports to one trusted, well-documented source that both people and AI tools can work from, bringing together 20 years of data from two incompatible systems. That work included:

  • the first data warehouse, reconciled to accounting and signed off by finance, with dashboards used across finance, marketing, customer experience and operations;
  • the first customer lifetime value model and cohort-based retention measures, which showed one region needed new customers, not better retention, and shifted its focus;
  • a low-cost test that ruled out a page redesign as the main fix for weak social sales;
  • a prototype AI assistant that answers business questions from dashboards people can check.

Why you can trust the work.

  • Every number traces to its source.
  • A clear “no” counts as much as a “yes”. Tests come with stop rules that save money.
  • Purpose before technology. Proven analytics where it delivers, AI where it opens doors that were closed before.
  • People keep the decisions. My job is to make the evidence clear.
